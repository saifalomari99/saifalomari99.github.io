## Overview

Magna's thermal sensing (night vision) system uses a front-mounted infrared camera that is sensitive to temperature differences of less than one tenth of a degree. Its algorithms detect animals, pedestrians, and cyclists more than 100 meters ahead of the vehicle to alert drivers of hazards, and can run in a dedicated ECU or be hosted by an OEM's central compute unit (CCU).

I work on the thermal camera program as an Embedded Firmware Engineer at Magna Electronics.

## Why thermal

As published by Magna, the system:

- helps drivers see up to four times the headlight range
- provides visibility through fog, smog, and smoke
- is not affected by the headlights of oncoming vehicles or glare from the sun

## Features (as published by Magna)

- Object classification: pedestrians, animals, vehicles, 2-wheeled vehicles, general objects
- Enhanced automated emergency braking
- Improved forward collision avoidance
- Day/night operation
- Thermal spotlighting
- Road edge classification
- Freespace

## Context

Since 2005, Magna has fielded over one million Night Vision systems on 50 vehicle lines, and states that its systems exceed current NCAP requirements for nighttime pedestrian crash avoidance. Magna describes ongoing development of better, smaller, lower-cost thermal cameras and new features, and positions thermal sensing as an essential sensing modality for safe autonomous vehicle operation.
